Start here: the 5-step client cycle

Use the templates in this order. Each step has one job; skipping one is where freelancers lose money.

The cycle

1. DISCOVER  - run the discovery call (Section 2), score the lead.
2. PROPOSE   - send the proposal within 24 hours (Section 3).
3. AGREE     - signed scope agreement + deposit BEFORE any work (Section 4).
4. DELIVER   - kickoff email, weekly updates, scope-creep scripts (Sections 5-7).
5. GET PAID  - invoice on delivery, follow up on a schedule (Section 8), then ask for a testimonial and referral (Section 10).

Rules that make the templates work:
- Never start without a deposit (30-50%).
- Put every change request in writing and price it.
- Invoice the day you deliver, not the day you remember.
- Fill every [BRACKET]. Delete anything that doesn't apply.

1. Price it right: three formulas

Most freelancers underprice because they guess. Use these.

Hourly rate from the income you want

HOURLY RATE = (target yearly income + yearly business costs) / billable hours per year

Realistic billable hours: 1,000-1,200 a year (about 20-24 a week). The rest is admin, sales and gaps.

Example: want $70,000, costs $8,000, 1,100 billable hours
($70,000 + $8,000) / 1,100 = $70.91 -> charge $75/hour as a floor.

Add 20-30% on top for taxes you set aside; the formula above is pre-tax income you keep.

Fixed project price

PROJECT PRICE = estimated hours x hourly rate x 1.25 (risk buffer)

Example: 20 hours x $75 x 1.25 = $1,875

Raise the buffer to 1.4 when: the client is new, the brief is vague, or there are many stakeholders.
Quote the price, not the hours. Hours invite haggling over your time instead of the value.

Three-tier offer (raises average sale)

Offer three options in the proposal:
GOOD   [$X]   - core deliverable only
BETTER [$1.5X] - core + [extra that saves them time]   <- the one you want them to pick
BEST   [$2.2X] - everything + [priority support / extra revision round / rush delivery]

Make BETTER the obvious value. Most clients choose the middle option.

Monthly retainer pricing

RETAINER = hours per month x hourly rate x 0.9 (small commitment discount)

Example: 10 hours x $75 x 0.9 = $675/month
Rules: unused hours don't roll over, billed on the 1st, 30 days notice to cancel.

2. Discovery call: questions and lead scoring

A good call decides whether to take the job and what to charge.

Questions to ask

THE PROBLEM
- What problem are we solving, and why now?
- What have you already tried? What happened?
THE RESULT
- What does success look like in 3 months? How will you measure it?
THE PEOPLE
- Who approves the work? Who else gives feedback? (More than 2 people = add buffer.)
THE LIMITS
- What is your deadline, and what drives it?
- What budget range have you set aside?
- What would make you regret hiring me?
THE HISTORY
- Have you worked with freelancers before? How did it go?
THE LOGISTICS
- What do you need to give me, and by when? (content, access, logins)

Lead score (add up, 0-10)

Give 2 points for each YES:
[ ] They can name a clear result they want
[ ] A budget was shared and fits your minimum
[ ] One decision maker, or 2 at most
[ ] A realistic deadline (not "yesterday")
[ ] They respect your time (on time, replied fast, no pushback on a deposit)

8-10: send the proposal today.
5-7: send it, add a 1.4 risk buffer, require 50% deposit.
0-4: politely pass or quote 2x. Template below.

Politely decline a bad-fit project

Subject: Thanks for thinking of me

Hi [CLIENT],

Thank you for the conversation about [PROJECT]. After thinking it over, I don't think I'm the right fit for this one because [timing / scope / budget alignment].

If it helps, I'd suggest [alternative resource or approach]. I'd be glad to look at future projects that fit my schedule.

Best,
[YOUR NAME]

4. Scope-of-work agreement

Plain English. Have a lawyer review for large projects; laws differ by location. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Agreement template

SCOPE OF WORK AGREEMENT
This agreement is between [YOUR NAME / BUSINESS] ("Freelancer") and [CLIENT / COMPANY] ("Client"), effective [DATE].

1. WORK. Freelancer will deliver [DELIVERABLES] as described in the proposal dated [DATE] (the "Work").
2. REVISIONS. [2] rounds of revisions are included. Extra rounds are billed at [$/hour].
3. CHANGES. Work outside this scope needs written approval and may change price and timeline.
4. FEES. Total fee [$X]. [50%] is due before work starts. The balance is due [14] days after delivery.
5. LATE PAYMENT. Balances unpaid after [14] days may incur [1.5%] monthly interest (or the maximum allowed by law). Work may pause until paid.
6. CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES. Client provides content, access and feedback within [3] business days. Delays may move the timeline.
7. OWNERSHIP. On full payment, Client owns the final deliverables. Until then Freelancer keeps ownership. Freelancer may show the work in a portfolio unless Client objects in writing.
8. THIRD-PARTY COSTS. Fees for stock, plugins, hosting or similar are paid by Client unless listed in the proposal.
9. CONFIDENTIALITY. Each party keeps the other's non-public information private.
10. TERMINATION. Either party may end this agreement with [7] days written notice. Client pays for work completed to that date. The deposit is non-refundable once work has started.
11. LIABILITY. Freelancer's liability is limited to the fees paid under this agreement. Freelancer is an independent contractor, not an employee.
12. ENTIRE AGREEMENT. This document and the proposal are the whole agreement. Changes must be in writing.

Freelancer: ____________  Date: ______
Client:     ____________  Date: ______

Why each clause matters (read once)

Revisions (2): without a number, "one small tweak" repeats forever.
Changes (3): your written protection against scope creep.
Late payment (5): late fees are rarely charged, but their existence speeds payment.
Ownership on payment (7): keeps leverage until you're paid.
Termination (10): lets you leave a bad client and keep your deposit.
Third-party costs (8): stops you paying for the client's tools.
Deposit: the best predictor of whether a client pays the rest.

6. Scope-creep and pushback scripts

Say yes to the relationship and no to unpaid work. Copy, edit, send.

'Can you also add this quick thing?'

Happy to. That's outside the current scope, so here is what it would add: [$ and time]. If you'd like it, reply "approved" and I'll fold it into the plan. If not, we'll keep to the current scope.

'Just one more revision'

We've used the [2] revision rounds in the agreement. I can do another round for [$] and have it back by [DATE]. Want me to go ahead?

'Can you do it cheaper?'

I understand budget matters. I can't lower the price for the same scope, but I can reduce the scope. Here are two options: [lower-priced option with fewer deliverables] or the original with a payment plan of [2/3 installments]. Which fits better?

'We'll pay you in exposure / after we get funding'

Thanks for thinking of me. I only take paid work with a deposit, so I'll pass for now. If the budget changes, I'd be glad to talk again.

'Can we do a quick call to discuss?' (when it's really free consulting)

Happy to help. For strategy and advice sessions I charge [$/hour], and I can send a calendar link. For the project itself, I'll keep updates in the weekly email.

Client is late with feedback

Hi [CLIENT], I'm waiting on [feedback/files] to continue. Based on the agreement, delays move the delivery date. If I get it by [DATE], I can still deliver on [DATE]; otherwise the new date is [DATE].

Client wants to start before paying the deposit

I'd love to start. I begin as soon as the deposit invoice is paid; I can have the first milestone to you by [DATE] after that.

8. Invoices and getting paid

Get paid on time without sounding rude.

Invoice wording

INVOICE #[NUMBER]   Date: [DATE]   Due: [DATE]
Bill to: [CLIENT]
Description: [PROJECT] - [deposit / final balance]
Amount: [$]
Pay by: [bank/link/payment method]
Terms: Net [14]. Late fee [1.5%]/month after due date.

Late-invoice email sequence

DAY 1 AFTER DUE
Subject: Quick check on invoice [#]
Hi [CLIENT], a friendly note that invoice [#] for [$AMOUNT] was due on [DATE]. Payment link: [LINK]. If it's already on its way, thank you!

DAY 7
Subject: Invoice [#] is a week overdue
Hi [CLIENT], I haven't received payment for invoice [#] ([$AMOUNT]). Can you confirm when I can expect it? If there's an issue with the invoice, tell me and I'll fix it today.

DAY 14
Subject: Action needed: invoice [#]
Hi [CLIENT], invoice [#] is now 14 days overdue. Per our agreement, work pauses and late fees begin on [DATE] unless payment arrives. I'd rather keep things moving. Can you pay by [DATE]?

DAY 30
Subject: Final notice: invoice [#]
Hi [CLIENT], this is my final notice for invoice [#] ([$AMOUNT] plus late fees of [$]). If payment isn't received by [DATE], I'll need to pursue collection options. Please reply today with a payment date.

Payment plan offer

Hi [CLIENT], I understand cash flow can be tight. I can split the balance into [3] payments of [$] due [DATE], [DATE] and [DATE]. Please confirm and I'll send the schedule.

Phone call script for a late client

'Hi [NAME], it's [YOU]. I'm following up on invoice [#] for [$], due [DATE]. I wanted to check if there's anything I can fix on my side. When can I expect payment?'
(Then stay quiet. Write down the date they give and email it back as confirmation.)

9. Raise your rates

Three versions, from softest to firmest.

Existing client, new rate

Subject: Updating my rates from [MONTH]

Hi [CLIENT],

I've enjoyed working together on [PROJECT/RESULT]. Starting [DATE], my rate for new work will be [$NEW] (currently [$OLD]). Projects already agreed stay at the current rate.

If you'd like to schedule upcoming work at the current rate, send me the details by [DATE] and I'll lock it in.

Thanks for the trust,
[YOUR NAME]

Retainer client

Hi [CLIENT], after [N] months of working together, my retainer will move from [$OLD] to [$NEW] per month starting [DATE]. You'll get [added value, e.g. faster turnaround / extra hours]. Please reply to confirm.

Client pushes back on the new rate

I understand. The new rate reflects [the results delivered / increased demand / my costs]. I can keep your current rate for [3 months] while we plan, or adjust the scope to fit your current budget. What works better?

When to raise

- You're booked more than 80% of the time
- You've won 3 proposals in a row without negotiation
- It's been 12 months since your last increase
Rule of thumb: raise new-client prices 15-25% when any of those is true; raise existing clients 10% a year.

10. After the project: testimonials, referrals, repeat work

The cheapest revenue is from clients who already trust you.

Testimonial request (day 7)

Subject: Quick favor?

Hi [CLIENT], I'm glad [PROJECT] is live. Would you share 2-3 sentences about the experience? A few prompts if useful:
- What problem did you have before?
- What changed after we worked together?
- Who would you recommend me to?
Thank you!

Referral request (day 14)

Hi [CLIENT], if you know anyone who could use help with [what you do], I'd be grateful for an introduction. I'm happy to send a short blurb you can forward. As a thanks, I'll give you [$ / % off / a free hour] for any referral that becomes a project.

Reactivate an old client (day 60-90)

Hi [CLIENT], it's been a few months since [PROJECT]. How's it going? I noticed [specific improvement idea]. If you're planning anything this quarter, I have availability from [DATE]. Happy to send a quick idea or two, no commitment.

Maintenance upsell

Hi [CLIENT], to keep [DELIVERABLE] working well, I offer a monthly plan: [what's included] for [$/month]. It covers [updates, fixes, report]. Want me to set it up?
